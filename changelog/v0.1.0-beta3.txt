FLOW DESKTOP CHANGE LOG
VERSION: 0.1.0-beta3
DATE: 2026-09-25
STATUS: PRE-RELEASE

ABOUT
Flow Desktop is a privacy-first YouTube and YouTube Music client - no account, no ads, no analytics, no telemetry. It is the desktop companion to Flow for Android.

MUSIC
- The music home opens instantly from a saved copy and refreshes in the background, and keeps showing your last feed when you are offline.
- New shelves built on your listening: Listen Again, Trending, a morning, evening or late-night rotation, Speed Dial with one-click shuffle, Rediscover, Deep Cuts, Artists for You, Popular artists, Mixed for you, genre mixes, a mood Vibes row and releases from your favorite artists.
- Charts follow the country set in Settings, Daily Mixes open as their own playable page, and a new Moods and genres browser lets you explore by feel.
- Similar to now mixes in artists and playlists and links to the artist, community playlists can be sampled right from the home, Quick Picks no longer fill up with chart hits or one artist, and "See all" opens the right page.
- Fixed related songs, Daily Mixes and the Similar to shelves coming up empty after YouTube Music added a Comments tab next to Related.
- Start a radio station from any song menu. It keeps playing for that session without changing your Autoplay setting, and switching Autoplay off stops it.
- Songs start in a few seconds instead of up to 15: cover art loads separately so it can no longer hold up the song, Quick Picks refresh when you open Home instead of on every song change, and radio lookups wait until the song is playing.
- Songs change with no gap: the next song is buffered before the current one ends, even while Flow is in the background.
- A song that stops loading recovers on its own and carries on from where it stopped, and Retry no longer restarts the song.
- Listening time is saved while Flow is in the background, so history and recommendations count those plays.
- Open lyrics stop redrawing once the song is paused and the lines have settled, and the song clock no longer runs a frame loop the whole time music plays. On Linux, lyrics fade instead of blurring.
- Normalize volume now works on every song, the Episodes search filter shows episodes instead of profiles, albums show their release year, and explicit badges show on album and playlist cards.
- Podcast episodes in music search are shown as episodes of their show instead of songs by an artist called "Episode", and play when clicked. Podcast cards open the show with its title, author and description.
- Profiles in music search are labelled Profile and open their channel page instead of an empty artist page. The top result keeps the songs listed under it.
- Music search only offers the categories YouTube Music has results for, and its filters use the parameters YouTube Music sends today.
- An artist's Shuffle plays YouTube Music's shuffle of their whole catalogue and Mix plays their station, instead of both reshuffling the few top songs on the page. Artist pages sort their shelves the same way in every language and link the sources under the bio.
- Songs added by radio keep their album, explicit badge and song or video type, and radio continues YouTube Music's own mix for the song. Lyrics from YouTube Music show their source.
- The full player stays dark in light themes, as it does on Android. Its title, controls and lyrics were dark text on a dark backdrop.
- The full player takes its colours from the cover the way Flow for Android does: a dark base and a vivid accent, each moved until it reads, so pale or grey covers no longer wash out the text. Colours ease over a second when the song changes.
- New Player settings: "Artwork colors on controls", with black and white or the app theme instead, and "Light in light theme" for a light full player. The Default Surface background now follows the app theme like on Android.
- On wide windows the full player keeps the song on the left and shows Up next or the lyrics beside it, like Flow for Android on tablets. Lyrics can still take the whole player. The artwork shrinks to fit short windows instead of pushing the controls off screen.
- The side pane can be put away with its hide button, or by pressing the Queue or Lyrics button again, and the song takes the whole player. Flow remembers this, and the Queue and Lyrics buttons bring the pane back.
- The full player answers the same keys as Flow for Android: Space or K to play and pause, J, L and the arrow keys to seek, Up and Down for volume, M to mute, Shift+N and Shift+P to skip, 0 to 9 to jump through the song, S, R and Q for shuffle, repeat and the queue. Escape closes an open menu before the player.
- The full player has its own volume button, the mouse wheel changes volume over either volume button, and Shift with the wheel seeks over the seek bar. Hovering Next shows the song that plays next, and the top shows what you are playing from.
- The song menu in the player adds Start radio, Equalizer, Not interested and Don't recommend this artist.
- New Immersive Artwork background for the full player: the cover fills the top, the left side of a wide window beside Up next or the lyrics, or the middle of one with that pane hidden, and fades into the player. The blurred backgrounds now load the cover through the same copy as the artwork, so they no longer stay black when the cover is slow.
- "Watch video" in the player's song menu opens the song's official music video at the same moment and pauses the song. The video is matched the same way Flow for Android does, so live takes, remixes and audio uploads are skipped.
- The full player's seek bar glides with the song instead of stepping, and dragging it seeks once when you let go instead of on every move. The page behind the full player stops drawing while it is covered.
- Mood and genre pages keep their shelves with titles instead of one long grid, album cards start playing without loading the album first, and playlist cards show their song count instead of the word Playlist.
- Shuffle now rearranges the queue, so every song plays once, Previous and Up next follow the shuffled order, and turning shuffle off puts the original order back.
- Radio keeps going after a shuffled queue ends, holds at most two songs per artist, and starts again from songs near the first one when YouTube's mix runs out. The queue says when the radio has run out.
- Removing a song or clearing the queue can be undone from the message that appears, and songs that already played can be added to the queue again.
- A song paused while it was still loading stays paused, and a recovery never starts a song you paused.
- A song whose link expires during long pauses recovers each time, waits for the connection to come back when the network drops, and tries another audio format when one cannot be decoded. Errors now say which of these went wrong.
- Media keys and the system's Now Playing controls follow whichever player started last, show the song's progress, and seek forward and back. Starting a video pauses the music and starting music pauses the video.
- Flow reopens the music player on the song you stopped at, paused at the same point, with its queue, shuffle order and radio. "Restore the last queue" in Settings turns this off.
- History and recommendations count the time a song actually played, so skipping or dragging to the end no longer counts as a full listen, and a short replay no longer replaces a longer one.
- A new "Resume long tracks" setting, off by default, starts mixes, podcasts and episodes of 10 minutes or more where you left them.
- Music controls in the menu bar or system tray: the current song on top, then Play or Pause, Previous, Next and Show Flow. Turn it off under Integrations with Show music controls in the tray.
- On Linux the tray is off by default and needs the libayatana-appindicator3 library, which the .deb, .rpm and AppImage now bring along. Without it the switch stays off instead of stopping Flow from starting.
- Scrobbling to Last.fm, Libre.fm and ListenBrainz, under Integrations. A song counts once you have heard half of it or four minutes, and songs of 30 seconds or less never count. Videos are never sent.
- Listens wait on your computer while you are offline and go out once you are back, up to 2,000 per service. Show now playing, Love liked songs and Scrobble during Deep Flow are options, and you can use your own Last.fm API key.
- Scrobbling no longer asks for your keychain password every time you open music settings or play a song. Flow reads the keychain only to send a listen while you are signed in, at most once per launch, and keeps all its scrobbling secrets in one keychain item.
- If you refuse keychain access, Flow stops asking and keeps your listens waiting. Settings shows an Allow access button to try again.
- Your Last.fm password is sent once and never stored. Sessions and tokens are kept in the system keychain, never in sync, backups or settings exports.
- Settings has a Music section with every music setting in one place: playback, audio and the equalizer, the full player's look, lyrics, the tray and scrobbling.
- Swipe a song in Up next to the left to play it next, or to the right to remove it, by dragging the row or with two fingers on a trackpad, as on Android. Settings › Music can change what each side does. The row menu has the same actions, Delete removes the focused row, and clicking the song that is playing no longer starts it over.
- Drag the music dock up, or swipe up on it with two fingers, and the full player grows out of it as on Android; it sinks back into the dock when it closes. The dock also has larger artwork with a playing animation and a clearer title.

AUDIO
- Equalizer boosts no longer distort: the music equalizer turns the whole curve down by its highest boost before the filters, and a soft limiter bends any peak that still goes past full scale.
- Loudness normalization for music can now be turned off in the equalizer panel. It stays on by default, as on Android.
- Downloaded songs keep their loudness value, so they play at the same level as streamed songs instead of louder. Songs downloaded before this update keep playing at full level.
- A new equalizer, the same as Flow for Android's: graphic and parametric modes, a response graph you can drag, per-band type, frequency, gain and Q, hold to compare, auto preamp or a manual preamp, and bass boost.
- Android's 13 presets with translated names, your own presets with Save, Save as, Rename, Duplicate and Delete with Undo, and a Custom entry that keeps the curve you tuned when you try a preset. AutoEQ and EqualizerAPO files import from a file or pasted text and export to a file or the clipboard.
- The equalizer is saved with your settings, included in backups and synced with Flow for Android. Your old equalizer gains carry over, with the 32 Hz and 16 kHz bands now peaks like on Android, and the panel says when audio processing is not available on your system.
- Music only goes through audio processing once the equalizer changes the sound, and playback resumes on its own after sleep or an output device change.
- Music has its own playback speed from 0.5x to 2x, with presets and the pitch kept, saved with your settings.
- The music volume slider follows how loud it sounds, so each step changes the level by a similar amount. Your saved volume plays at the same level after the update.
- Music loudness is measured the way Android does it, from the perceptual loudness first, and loud songs are never turned down by more than 20 dB.
- An optional crossfade, off by default, fades the next song in over 1 to 12 seconds. Consecutive tracks from the same album and repeat-one keep their gapless joins.
- The equalizer now also applies to videos, including the pop-out player, and opens from the video settings menu. Shorts are left as they are.
- Videos only go through audio processing once the equalizer changes the sound, and only after a short silent self-test shows it works. Where it does not, as on some Linux setups, videos keep playing normally and the menu says the equalizer is not available.
- On Windows, the volume mixer now lists Flow by its own name and icon instead of "Microsoft Edge WebView2", including after you switch to another output device.

MACOS
- Flow uses the native macOS window controls and titlebar again. Closing the window hides it and keeps playback and downloads running until you quit, and clicking the Dock icon brings it back.
- The window background now follows your theme. A Tauri bug was clearing it, which showed a mismatched color around the app while resizing.
- On 120 Hz ProMotion screens Flow now draws at 120 frames per second. WebKit was holding every animation, scroll and lyric line to 60.
- The music equalizer and crossfade are now heard on macOS. WebKit cannot pass Opus songs through audio processing, so once either is on, songs stream as AAC and the song playing reloads once from where it is.
- The volume slider keeps working on a downloaded Opus song while the equalizer is on.
- AAC songs now show and play for their real length on macOS. WebKit reported them at twice their length, so a song ran on in silence before the next one started.

LINUX
- The AppImage is built on Ubuntu 24.04 with a newer WebKitGTK that no longer crashes on color emoji fonts, the cause of the freeze on Fedora 44 and Bazzite. It needs glibc 2.39 or newer, so on Ubuntu 22.04, Mint 21 or Debian 12 use the .deb instead.
- The AppImage drops its bundled Wayland libraries on both x64 and ARM64 again, which avoids the EGL_BAD_PARAMETER startup crash on newer Mesa.
- The AppImage uses your system's VA-API libraries, so hardware video decoding can work with your installed drivers.
- Flow remembers which graphics workaround last worked instead of dropping back to none every other launch, and starts NVIDIA systems on Wayland with the DMABUF renderer off.
- A new Graphics compatibility setting under Appearance, and a "Start with safe graphics" launcher action, help when the window opens blank.
- If the page inside the window crashes, Flow reloads it instead of freezing, and quits after three crashes in a minute so the next launch starts clean.
- Programs Flow starts from the AppImage no longer load the AppImage's own libraries, and the startup log reports its bundled video decoders correctly.
- Thumbnails, avatars and artwork load in the AppImage on openSUSE, Fedora and other distros where they showed blank. They now come through Flow's own connection, which finds your system's certificates.
- The .deb, .rpm and AppImage now find Flow's bundled poToken and signature scripts, which they were looking for in the wrong folder. With Node installed, the backup playback clients that need them now work.
- Flow no longer offers in-app updates when Flatpak or your package manager installed it, or when it runs as a plain binary. Settings says what handles updates instead, and these installs stop adding a second flow:// link handler.
- Arch Linux packaging for the AUR: flow-desktop builds Flow from source and flow-desktop-bin installs the release .deb. Both use your system's WebKitGTK and Node, and start with the flow-desktop command so they don't clash with the AUR packages that already use flow.
- Nightly builds now include a Flatpak (#91), built from source on the GNOME 51 runtime with its own sandbox permissions and app data. It is the first step toward a Flathub release.
- The last-resort graphics fallback only switches to X11 when an X server is running, so it no longer stops the window from opening on Wayland without XWayland or inside the Flatpak.
- Open in browser, links in descriptions and on channel pages, Open logs folder and Show in folder work in the AppImage (#141). Flow starts your system's own launcher there instead of the AppImage's copy, which failed without a word.
- When no browser opens, Open in browser says so and offers to copy the link instead.

INTERFACE
- Saving, liking or adding a video, Short, song, album or playlist shows a toast with its poster and title, names where it went, and offers View and Undo. A failed save names the item and offers Retry.
- Toasts slide in and out, stay up longer when they have buttons, pause while you point at them, and sit above the music player bar instead of covering its buttons.
- Right-clicking or opening the three-dot menu on a video, Short, playlist, song or album shows a redesigned menu: the item's poster and title at the top, its main actions as quick buttons, and the rest grouped, with removals in red and arrow-key navigation. Videos gain Share.
- Menus, dropdown lists and the search suggestions open and close the same way everywhere: they grow quickly from the corner next to their button and fade out when closed, instead of popping in and vanishing. Closing one by keyboard puts focus back on its button.
- Resizing the window is smoother: video grids and the lyrics view re-measure once per frame instead of on every resize event.
- Copying or sharing a video, Short, chapter, song or post gives a real YouTube link instead of Flow's own address, and Shorts links now include the video.
- Share buttons open the system share sheet where there is one and copy the link where there isn't. A copy that fails now says so instead of showing "copied".
- The search box has a new look and richer suggestions: the part you typed stays plain and the rest is bold, well-known people and places show their picture and a short description, and an arrow puts a suggestion into the box to keep editing.
- Songs, artists, albums and videos from YouTube Music appear beside the suggestions with their covers and avatars, and open or play straight away.
- Searches are remembered on this computer and shown when you click the empty search box, with a button to remove one or clear them all. Deep Flow keeps searches out of it unless history is allowed there. Ctrl K shows as Cmd K on macOS.
- A maximized or fullscreen window no longer shows a resize cursor along its edges.
- The collapsed side rail shows icons only, with each name in a tooltip, a little more room between them, and Sync, Extensions and Support at the bottom. Opening or closing it now slides smoothly and pushes the page instead of swapping at once. The top bar button shows a panel open or close icon to match.
- Moving to another page, or another Settings section, fades the new one in with a small rise instead of swapping it at once. The top bar, side rail and players stay still, and the watch page only fades.
- The menu on the watch and settings pages slides in and out, sits right under the top bar on macOS, closes when you leave the page or press Escape, and moves keyboard focus into the menu.
- Artist, album and playlist headers, the music player background, playlist cards and blocked-video cards draw their soft artwork once instead of blurring it on every repaint. Player menus and overlays use solid backgrounds instead of blurring what is behind them.
- The home feed keeps loading new videos as you scroll instead of stopping after a few pages. Each search now continues to its next page of results instead of repeating the first one.
- New search topics are only picked once the current ones run out of results, so the feed no longer reruns the same searches every few seconds at the bottom.
- When there is nothing new left, the home feed says so and offers a Refresh button instead of retrying forever.
- Video cards show a verified tick next to channels, YouTube's own badges such as 4K, CC and New, a Members only label, and an Upcoming badge on premieres instead of a waiting count read as views.
- A Short found in search or a channel opens in the Shorts player, and collaboration videos open the lead channel instead of none. Related cards show channel avatars.
- A new Content language setting has YouTube write dates, view counts, labels and its translated titles in one of 75 languages. Upload dates are read in that language, so Home still knows how new each video is. Playback stays as it was.
- Video cards on standard-resolution screens load a smaller thumbnail when the card is small enough for it, and switch to the sharper one in wide layouts. Playlist headers fall back to a sharp thumbnail when the largest one is missing.
- Open Collective is now the main way to support Flow, ahead of Patreon, on the Support page, in Settings > About and in the occasional support reminder. Open Collective lists every contribution and expense publicly.
- Shorts fill the window's height on large screens instead of stopping at 850 pixels, and still fit beside the comments on narrow or portrait windows. Long channel names get the full width, with Subscribe moved under the name.
- Video titles, descriptions, comments, live chat, channel names and About pages, posts, the song title in the music player and the Diagnostics log can now be selected and copied with Ctrl+C or Cmd+C.
- Right-clicking selected text offers Copy and Search Flow for it. Selecting part of a description no longer opens or closes it.
- The Subscribe button eases between Subscribe and Subscribed, shows a ringing bell when the channel's notifications are on and rings it once when they turn on. Its menu offers All or None, and says so when notifications are off for every channel in Settings.
- Unsubscribing shows a toast with Undo, which brings the channel back with its groups.

TABS AND WINDOWS
- Going Back returns to where you were on the page, such as halfway down Home, instead of the top.
- The mouse side buttons, Alt+Left and Alt+Right (and Cmd+[ and Cmd+] on a Mac) go back and forward. The Back and Forward buttons dim when there is nowhere to go.
- Each tab keeps its own video. Switching away from a tab that is playing keeps the video going in the mini player, and the mini player's expand button takes you back to that tab.
- Opening a video in another tab pauses the first one where it was; going back to its tab picks it up from that spot.

PLAYER
- Hovering the seek bar shows a preview frame of that moment, and the most-replayed graph above it shows which parts people watch again.
- Stable volume now works: it plays YouTube's stable-volume audio, which evens out loud and quiet parts, on videos that have it. A new Normalize volume across videos setting turns down videos mastered louder than YouTube's target.
- Fewer "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" errors: when YouTube asks, the player gets a fresh visitor session and tries again before moving on.
- Playback now tries the same chain of YouTube clients as Flow for Android: visionOS first, then mobile web and web through SABR streaming, then the Android VR builds, then the Android app.
- Videos opened right after launch no longer time out on slow connections: playback requests get their own connection instead of queuing behind the home and subscription feeds.
- Live streams no longer fail to refresh on slow connections, and a video that no longer exists now shows its own message instead of a generic error.
- Subtitles look and behave like YouTube's: auto-generated captions roll up two lines as words are spoken, and manual captions keep all their lines.
- Subtitles scale with the player, with a size setting from 50% to 400% in Settings, Subtitle Style or the + and - keys. Saved subtitle sizes and positions from older builds reset to the defaults.
- Drag subtitles anywhere on the player for the current video. Clicking them still pauses, seeks or opens the menu as before.
- The player's settings menu and right-click menu close when you click the video, the control bar or anywhere else, or press Escape, and that click no longer pauses the video. Only one of them is open at a time.
- Both menus have a cleaner look and open and close quickly without bouncing. The settings menu eases its height and slides between its pages, and the right-click menu opens at the cursor. Their labels can now be translated.
- Theater mode is lighter: the color glow around the video is drawn without a blur filter, samples the video less often, and can be turned off with the new Ambient mode setting. It is off on Linux, where it cost the most.
- The player follows the page while you scroll without re-laying out the window, and scrolling a menu or side panel no longer moves it.
- Theater mode now glides: the player grows into its new place and back, its corners squaring as it goes, while the page below fades in. Comments, live chat and the side panels keep their place instead of loading again. If a computer is too slow for the glide, the player fades instead.
- The seek bar and time stop redrawing every frame while the video is paused or the controls are hidden.
- Web playback clients send their playback token where YouTube reads it, videos behind a content warning get their related videos and autoplay back, live streams show how many are watching, and hidden like counts no longer read "I like this".
- Flow keeps one anonymous YouTube identity for up to a week instead of a new one every launch, and gets a new one at most twice in six hours after a bot check. Diagnostics can reset it.
- YouTube requests give up after 20 seconds instead of hanging, retry briefly after a dropped connection, and say when the connection failed or YouTube asked Flow to slow down.
- An upcoming premiere or live stream shows a countdown and starts on its own when it goes live, and members-only videos, rate limits and networks YouTube is blocking each get their own message instead of a generic error.
- A video that stops partway through gets fresh links and resumes where it stopped before switching sources, a client YouTube starts refusing is tried last for half an hour, and streams no longer stop after an hour.
- The audio picker takes the normal copy over its volume-compressed twin, subtitles translate from the authored track under one Auto-translate menu, and live playlists download compressed.
- Music skips a removed or region-locked song instead of stopping, at most three in a row.
- Videos YouTube serves only to web players play in installed builds without Node, because the player script now runs in a hidden window.
- A video the app clients only offer below 720p now plays in HD from the web clients when YouTube has it there.
- A new Remote signature fallback setting in Network, off by default, lets Flow ask PipePipe's public decoder to unlock the TV app's streams when the usual sources stop working.
- The watch page loads its details, related videos, first comments and chat from one YouTube request instead of four, and no longer loads the whole channel page just for its avatar and subscriber count.
- Chapters come from YouTube with their thumbnails, including the ones YouTube generates, and a description with stray timestamps no longer turns into fake chapters.
- In fullscreen, chapters with the transcript, the queue and live chat open as a panel on the right. The video narrows to make room instead of being covered, and Escape closes the panel before it leaves fullscreen.
- A chapters or queue panel opened in fullscreen is still open when you leave it, and the transcript no longer loads again when the panel moves.
- Collaborations show every channel, each with its own subscribe button, and the watch page shows hashtags, badges like Unlisted, the artist badge and more related videos on request.
- Finished live streams replay their chat in step with the video, live chat can switch between Top and All messages, and autoplay starts with the video YouTube itself would play next.
- Links in descriptions and comments open in the app when they point at YouTube: videos, playlists and channels open directly, hashtags search, timestamps seek, and other links open in the browser.
- Expanding a description shows its headline numbers, the songs used in the video, which play in the music player, and the creator's own links.
- Comments show who pinned or hearted them, mark the creator and verified channels, can be sorted by Top or Newest, show the exact count, and say when the creator turned comments off.
- Search shows the Shorts, the "Latest from" shelves, YouTube playlists and channel subscriber counts it used to drop, and Shorts discovery finds many more real Shorts.
- Search filters work as YouTube applies them: relevance or view count, the right length buckets, a type filter, several features at once, and suggestions for your region.
- A misspelt search says what it searched for instead and can search the original, collaborations show every channel on their cards, and onboarding finds about 20 channels per search.
- Explore works again: YouTube retired the Trending page Flow used, so Explore now shows YouTube's Live, Gaming, Music, News, Sports, Learning and Fashion & Beauty pages for your region, with News categories and a See all grid that keeps loading as you scroll.
- Channel pages show the channel's own Home shelves in its order, including its trailer, featured channels and collaborations, with a See all on each. The header shows the handle, subscriber and video counts, and the description opens an About panel with links, join date and views.
- Channel tabs come from the channel itself, with its own sort and filter options on Videos, Live and Playlists. Posts show every picture of a gallery, poll results and quizzes that reveal the answer when you pick one.
- Pasted @handle, /c/ and /user/ links open the right channel, and Watched videos on a channel only lists videos from that channel.
- Subscriptions no longer lose a channel's uploads when its feed fails to load: Flow retries once, then reads the channel's Videos tab, and names any channel it still could not read with a Try again button.
- Shorts in Subscriptions are taken from each channel's own Shorts tab instead of guessed from length, so long Shorts and short videos land in the right place. Premieres show as upcoming instead of "Just now", members-only uploads are left out, and cards show view counts.
- The Shorts feed's subscription lane finds your channels' Shorts again; it was asking for their Videos tab.
- The Shorts feed loads its reels in pages about a hundred times smaller, drops the ads mixed into them, and gives them their real titles and channels so your interests and blocked channels apply to them.
- One sleep timer now serves both the video and the music player, with Flow for Android's choices: 10 to 90 minutes, a custom time, End of song, and Quit Flow when it ends, which is remembered.
- Music fades out over the last 20 seconds before the timer stops it, and the fade keeps going while Flow is in the background.
- A Sleep timer button can be added next to Like in the full music player from Settings, Player.
- Clicking a related video, Next or autoplay switches straight to the new video. Before, the old one loaded again for a moment, which could restart its opening, leave a stale position or clear its title in History.
- A quality change in progress when the video changes can no longer move the new video to the old one's position, and the music seek bar no longer shows the last song's time while the next one loads.
- A long video that stops and recovers carries on from where it stopped instead of the beginning, and so does Retry. A source change no longer records 0 as your place in the video.
- A video can fetch fresh links on its own up to three times, each after at least five more minutes of playback, and videos resume at your place directly instead of loading the opening first.
- A source that never starts loading is dropped after 30 seconds for the next one, and fetching fresh links shows the loading spinner and gives up after a minute. When nothing plays, the player now says so instead of spinning forever or going blank.
- The player keeps 30 seconds of video behind the playhead instead of as much as it buffers ahead, which used up to two extra minutes of video in memory.
- Videos streamed through SABR no longer stop an hour after they started, and fetching fresh links gives them a new SABR session instead of the expired one.
- A VPN or network change no longer pushes Flow's most reliable playback client to the back of the line for half an hour. Only a link refused within minutes of being issued counts against its client.
- The pop-out player window moves when you drag the video itself, not only the strip along its top (#140). Holding the video still speeds it up, and a click still pauses.
- Long Press Speed has an Off choice that turns hold-to-speed-up off in the player, the pop-out window and Shorts.
- Leaving the page of a paused video shrinks it into the mini player, still paused, instead of closing it, the same as a playing video.
- The quality switch notice and the loading spinner over the video are smaller and quieter, and a late time update from the previous video can no longer land on the next one.
- The queue panel names the playlist you are playing, shows your place in it and links back to it. During a playlist, Add to queue plays the video next, and with nothing playing it starts the video in the mini player.
- Turning shuffle off puts the queue back in its original order. Queue rows drag to reorder in any queue. Removing a video in the queue also removes it from Watch Later or your own playlist, with Undo, and drags in Manual order are saved to the playlist.

SYNC
- Syncing with a phone works in every direction again: the desktop handles collections it doesn't know yet, like Notes on newer Android builds, files each collection by name, and no longer hangs when it scans a phone's send code.
- Pairing codes no longer show as expired on the phone when the two devices' clocks disagree, and codes that point outside your local network are refused.
- The connection stays up while you read the merge prompt, the other device only reports success once the data is saved, and stalled sessions time out instead of hanging.
- Sync errors say what went wrong and what to try, the pairing screen can copy the connection data for a device without a camera, and it suggests firewall checks when nothing connects.
- Removing a video from history or unliking it on the desktop now reaches Flow for Android instead of coming back on the next sync. Deleted playlists, removed playlist tracks and deleted subscription groups reach your other desktops.
- Renaming a playlist no longer leaves a copy behind on the other device, and playlists with the same name on two devices are matched once and stay matched.
- Settings sync with Flow for Android under the names it understands, and a newer change made on the desktop is no longer overwritten. The video codec preference now stays on each device.
- Recommendation counts no longer grow on every sync, and a channel, topic or artist you unblock on the desktop stays unblocked. Counts that had already grown are corrected once.

RECOMMENDATIONS
- Topics are read from titles the way Flow for Android reads them, so "Guitars" and "guitar" are one interest and a synced profile no longer splits one word into several topics.
- Your existing recommendation profile is converted once on the first start, keeping what it learned without counting anything twice.
- Interests, time patterns and discovery searches show topics the way titles write them instead of as shortened word stems.

LYRICS
- BetterLyrics no longer switches off for 10 minutes after a song it has not seen before, so word-by-word lyrics stop disappearing.

- Lyrics are looked up once per song as soon as it starts, so they are usually ready when you open them. Word-by-word lyrics win over line-by-line ones found a moment earlier.
- Lyrics saved with a download show at once, with their timing, instead of behind a spinner.
- Two new lyrics sources: lrc.red, second in line, brings word-by-word lyrics for many songs BetterLyrics has not seen, and Unison, eighth, has lyrics people submit themselves.
- The lyrics providers in Settings can be reordered by dragging them or with their arrow buttons.
- Lyrics requests only go to the lyrics providers and give up after 8 seconds, so a lookup that was left behind no longer keeps running.
- The lyrics view is a port of Flow for Android's: lines spring into place one after another, the sung word lifts and fills behind a soft edge, long notes glow, and instrumental breaks show three breathing dots. Only the sung line redraws, and nothing moves while the song is paused or the window is hidden.
- Lyrics show their translation and romanization under each line, duets put each singer on their own side, background vocals stay with their line, Arabic and Hebrew read right to left, and line-by-line lyrics fill word by word.
- A new lyrics menu adjusts the timing, picks another source, edits, copies or saves the lyrics as an .lrc file, and sets their alignment.
- Lyrics found for a song are kept as files in Flow's app data instead of browser storage, which could fill up and stop saving them. Settings shows how much space they take and can clear them. Lyrics saved by earlier versions move over on their own.
- Downloaded songs get the timed lyrics the lyrics sources found, saved as a real .lrc file, instead of YouTube's plain text. A downloaded .lrc file's offset tag sets the starting timing.
- A downloaded song's lyrics show offline even without an .lrc file, read from inside the song file.
- Choose lyrics source lists the copy saved with the download first, next to the online sources. Picking it keeps it for that song with no online lookup; picking an online source looks online first and falls back to the saved copy. Refresh goes back to choosing automatically.
